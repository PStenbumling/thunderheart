# C — Character Visual Asset Pack

Production-ready character art direction for the THUNDERHEART universe, derived from the established story bible, character canon, and visual direction.

---

## A) Character Visual Bible

### 1. The Last Stormkeeper

**Silhouette summary**

Tall, broad-shouldered, and unmistakably vertical against the sky.  
Bare forearms break the armor mass and keep the figure human, not kingly.  
Raised straight sword and storm halo create a spear-like upward silhouette.

**Face and hair notes**

Long dark hair, storm-whipped and uneven rather than groomed. Face should read battle-worn, severe, and tired without looking old or fragile. Eyes carry internal amber-gold fire, but the glow should feel like burdened endurance, not rage.

**Clothing/armor notes**

Dark leather and fur armor with weather damage, layered for function, not ornament. No polished plate, no heraldry, no royal insignia. Torso protection should look hand-repaired over many campaigns.

**Prop/weapon notes**

Straight ancient sword, scarred blade, simple guard, visibly old enough to have passed through ages. Optional runestone nearby. No shield, no ornate relic clutter.

**Lighting cues**

Primary light from overhead blue-white storm energy. Secondary amber fill from distant ruins or firelight. Thunder Crown only at full-power moments and always as fractured arcs, never a neat ring.

**Signature pose**

On high rock, body turned three-quarters from camera, sword raised at roughly 80 degrees into the storm, gaze fixed above the horizon rather than at the viewer.

**5 never-do-this mistakes**

1. Do not give him regal plate armor or a kingly cape.
2. Do not center him symmetrically like a poster hero.
3. Do not make the Thunder Crown look like a metal crown.
4. Do not make him youthful or pretty-faced.
5. Do not place him in a valley, hall, or flat battlefield.

### 2. The New Bearer

**Silhouette summary**

Leaner, less settled, and slightly asymmetrical from found armor pieces.  
Sword lineage links them to the Stormkeeper, but posture is less absolute.  
Electric arcs should appear as nervous accents, not a completed halo.

**Face and hair notes**

Keep identity open enough to feel universal. Younger features, less weathered, fear and resolve visible at once. Hair can be medium-length and wind-pulled, but not as mythically iconic as the Stormkeeper's.

**Clothing/armor notes**

Mixed scavenged armor, practical layers, worn cloak or shoulder piece, mismatched plates and leather. Nothing regal, nothing custom-forged. The outfit should suggest someone who inherited destiny before they inherited proper gear.

**Prop/weapon notes**

The same ancestral sword, recognizable in silhouette. Optional satchel strap, travel wraps, or repaired gauntlet to sell the journey. No ceremonial extras.

**Lighting cues**

Electric Blue arcs should be small and unstable. Northern Star light can appear as colder, quieter guidance than the storm itself. Warmth should be limited and earned.

**Signature pose**

Mid-step on a ridge, sword lowered but ready, looking toward distant light with uncertainty rather than triumph.

**5 never-do-this mistakes**

1. Do not make them a polished chosen-one prince or princess.
2. Do not give them a full Thunder Crown yet.
3. Do not over-age them into a veteran.
4. Do not make the armor matching or luxurious.
5. Do not turn the expression into effortless confidence.

### 3. The Ashguard Lord

**Silhouette summary**

Heavy, iron-dense, and grounded like a moving fortress.  
Mass should sit low and forward, emphasizing pressure rather than speed.  
No crown effect, no radiance, only oppressive shape and intent.

**Face and hair notes**

Older than the New Bearer by a generation. Human eyes only, cold and decided, with no supernatural glow. Hair, if visible, should be restrained and functional; face marked by control, not theatrical madness.

**Clothing/armor notes**

Heavy black-iron armor built for war, scarred and functional. Minimal decoration, severe lines, deep black and blood-iron tones. The suit should communicate doctrine and refusal rather than vanity.

**Prop/weapon notes**

Brutal practical weaponry, such as a long warblade or heavy commander’s blade. Visible scar across the right hand. No mystical relics, no blue energy.

**Lighting cues**

Charcoal, ember amber, and blood iron dominate. Light is directional, hard, and hostile. If blue appears at all, it should be only as distant threat spill from the bearer, never as his own language.

**Signature pose**

Advancing through smoke with weapon low and ready, shoulders square, head slightly inclined as if dismissing fear.

**5 never-do-this mistakes**

1. Do not give him glowing magic eyes.
2. Do not make the armor ornate high-fantasy villain cosplay.
3. Do not add a crown, halo, horns, or demonic silhouette.
4. Do not make him chaotic or screaming by default.
5. Do not let blue become a dominant color in his scenes.

### 4. Lyraneth

**Silhouette summary**

Cloaked, upright, and composed, with authority carried in stillness.  
Map, cloak folds, and layered travel gear create a scholar-traveler outline.  
Her silhouette should read leadership without battlefield bulk.

**Face and hair notes**

Late 50s, lined face, sharp intelligence, tired but unbroken. Grey-streaked dark hair, practical and wind-aware rather than styled. Eyes should suggest long memory and immediate judgment.

**Clothing/armor notes**

Heavy travel cloak with Stonewatch star-thread worked subtly into the hem. Underlayers practical for hard roads and cold ruins. No armor glamour; she is protected by knowledge, endurance, and presence.

**Prop/weapon notes**

Ancient map as primary prop, possibly rolled, partially opened, or held under one arm. No weapon. Optional star-marked satchel, carved tokens, or weathered field journal.

**Lighting cues**

Frost Star rim light and quiet amber edge light work better than explosive lightning. When storm light touches her, it should read as reflected fate, not personal power.

**Signature pose**

Standing against the wind with map in hand, looking not at the camera but toward the route ahead, one hand securing the cloak.

**5 never-do-this mistakes**

1. Do not make her youthful or ethereal.
2. Do not arm her like a ranger or battle-mage.
3. Do not overload the costume with ornate fantasy trim.
4. Do not turn her into a mystical oracle with glowing hands.
5. Do not make her frantic in action scenes.

### 5. Aldric

**Silhouette summary**

Broad chest, compact aggression, protective angle toward incoming threat.  
Twin short blades create a distinct split-hand shape.  
He should look like a wall that learned how to move fast.

**Face and hair notes**

Weathered veteran, scarred, grounded, believable. Expression often grim with flashes of hard-earned humor. Hair and beard, if present, should be practical and rough, not baroque.

**Clothing/armor notes**

Functional soldier gear with patched leather, battered plates, and travel grime. Weight distribution should support fast close fighting. No noble insignia, no ceremonial finish.

**Prop/weapon notes**

Twin short blades, visibly used and maintained. Sheaths should be accessible and practical. Optional strap kit, bandages, or scavenged armor elements that suggest years in the field.

**Lighting cues**

Storm spill and firelight both work on him because he stands between danger and the bearer. His lighting should emphasize solidity over mysticism.

**Signature pose**

One blade forward, one back, torso angled between the bearer and the nearest attacker, knees bent as if bracing for impact.

**5 never-do-this mistakes**

1. Do not replace the twin blades with a greatsword.
2. Do not make him sleek or aristocratic.
3. Do not erase the scars and field wear.
4. Do not pose him as a solitary glory-seeker.
5. Do not make him visually more mythic than the bearer.

### 6. Seravyn

**Silhouette summary**

Lean, spare, and ghost-quiet, with little wasted shape.  
Draped ash-toned layers make the body feel half-withdrawn from the world.  
One hand near the chest becomes her recurring emotional silhouette mark.

**Face and hair notes**

Ageless in the unsettling sense, somewhere between 40 and ancient. Quiet sorrow, restraint, and intelligence must lead. Hair should be subdued, wind-touched, and unadorned.

**Clothing/armor notes**

Faded grey-brown garments, layered for walking and weather, almost monastic but never formal. Fabrics should feel repeatedly mended. No armor fantasy glamor, no saintly whiteness.

**Prop/weapon notes**

Keep props minimal. A walking staff, satchel, or wrapped relic fragment can work, but emptiness suits her. The true prop is the habitual hand-to-chest gesture.

**Lighting cues**

Muted storm ambience, pale star light, very limited warmth. She should feel like someone the storm once touched and then left aching.

**Signature pose**

Paused in motion, head slightly bowed, one hand unconsciously over the chest, eyes turned to weather arriving from far away.

**5 never-do-this mistakes**

1. Do not make her flamboyant or seductive.
2. Do not give her active lightning powers.
3. Do not turn her into a cheerful mentor.
4. Do not overfill her design with accessories.
5. Do not make her posture bold and victorious.

### 7. Thoran

**Silhouette summary**

Ancient, massive in stillness, like a carved outcrop rather than a man.  
Seated or standing, his shape should feel rooted and immovable.  
Staff, robes, or stone surroundings should merge him with the landscape.

**Face and hair notes**

Very old, deeply lined, skin and beard shaped by weather and time. One eye clouded, one eye sharp and searching. His expression is patient, grave, and stronger than his body.

**Clothing/armor notes**

Stonewatch monk layers, thick wool, rough cloth, ancient wraps, almost geological in texture. No glossy ceremonial robes. He should look like someone who has outlived comfort.

**Prop/weapon notes**

Staff, prayer stones, or seated stone bench are enough. He does not need a combat weapon. Directional facing toward Highrock is more important than gear.

**Lighting cues**

Low, cold ambient light with selective storm accents. A faint amber edge can suggest the last warmth of human continuity. His scenes should feel quiet, not explosive.

**Signature pose**

Seated facing the unseen Highrock, body still, chin lifted slightly, sharp eye fixed on distance no one else can read.

**5 never-do-this mistakes**

1. Do not make him frail in spirit.
2. Do not smooth out his age into generic wise-old-man fantasy.
3. Do not give him flashy magic effects.
4. Do not dress him in clean temple luxury.
5. Do not stage him as comic relief or eccentric mystic.

## D) Consistency Guardrails

**Recurring symbols and placement**

- **Heart-bolt symbol**: Carve into runestones, scabbard fittings, map corners, ruined thresholds, and occasional worn armor marks. Keep it legible and simple: heart body, lightning bisect, restrained gold edge.
- **Thunder Crown effect**: Only full bearers at full-power moments. Complete expression belongs to The Last Stormkeeper; The New Bearer gets partial unstable arcs only. Never place it on The Ashguard Lord, Lyraneth, Aldric, Seravyn, or Thoran.
- **Northern Star**: Small, distant, frost-cold point light at the same sky position when visible. Best used in New Bearer, Lyraneth, Seravyn, and Thoran shots. Never oversized.
- **Runestone motif**: Knee-height weathered markers, lower-right anchor when composition allows, lightly carved with the heart-bolt and storm scars. Use especially in Stormkeeper and Bearer scenes, and occasionally in Stonewatch scenes.

**Cross-character consistency checks**

1. Keep all materials weathered, used, and practical. No one should look freshly outfitted.
2. Storm light always reads as the world’s dominant supernatural language; blue belongs to the Heart and its orbit, not generic magic.
3. Gold is sparse and meaningful. Use it for inner flame, distant windows, legacy accents, or earned warmth, never for decorative luxury.
4. Composition should favor elevation, diagonal energy, and sky dominance. Heroic figures belong above the horizon line.
5. The Fallen Crown ruin language should repeat across multiple shots: dark silhouette, sparse amber windows, haunted distance.
6. The sword lineage between The Last Stormkeeper and The New Bearer must be visibly continuous in silhouette and wear.
7. Stonewatch design language should be subtle, thread-based, carved, and archival, never flashy religious fantasy.
8. Ashguard visual language should stay iron-heavy, human, and ideological. Avoid monsters unless explicitly designing troops outside this pack.
9. Expressions across all characters must remain emotionally serious and mythically restrained. Avoid modern sarcasm-face, comic-book exaggeration, or glam posing.
10. Painterly cinematic texture is mandatory across the whole set. Even turnaround sheets should feel like premium concept boards, not flat clip-sheet output.
